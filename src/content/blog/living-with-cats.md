Cats have lived alongside people for around ten thousand years, and in all that time they have never quite agreed to be domesticated. They moved in because our grain stores attracted mice, and they stayed because the arrangement suited them. It still does.

## What they need

A cat’s needs are simple, but they are not optional:

- **Fresh water**, ideally away from the food bowl. Many cats drink more from a fountain or a wide dish.
- **A clean litter tray**, in a quiet spot. A cat that stops using the tray is usually telling you something.
- **Somewhere high.** Shelves, the top of a wardrobe, a cat tree — height means safety and a good view.
- **Something to hunt.** Ten minutes with a feather toy each day does more for a bored indoor cat than any new bed.

## How they talk

Adult cats rarely meow at each other. They mostly save it for us, having learned that people respond to noise. The rest of their language is quieter: a slow blink means trust, a tail held straight up means a friendly greeting, and a twitching tail tip means you should probably stop what you are doing.

## What they give back

A cat will not fetch your slippers or come when called, at least not reliably. What it offers instead is company on its own terms — a warm weight on your lap in the evening, chosen rather than trained. That is the whole appeal.
